\section{Scientific question addressed}
A recent comparative study (Preethi et al., \emph{SN Computer Science}, 2025) reported that
Support Vector Regression ranks last among the regression algorithms it compared on the
California Housing benchmark. This paper asks a precise question about that specific result:
\emph{is the reported outcome a property of the algorithm, or of the configuration in which
the algorithm was run?}

The question is one of evaluation methodology and reproducibility in applied AI. It is
answered by reproduction rather than by assertion: the configuration described in the prior
study is re-run directly, components are added to it one at a time, and the resulting
attribution is tested for robustness to the order in which those components are introduced.

\section{Methodology}
The workflow is defined as an explicit nine-stage procedure (S1--S9) with stated inputs and
outputs, illustrated as a pipeline diagram in the manuscript. Every stage that estimates
anything from data --- feature ranking, scaler assignment, scaler parameters, hyperparameters
--- is fitted on the training portion only and re-fitted from scratch inside every fold of
every resampling scheme. The evaluation comprises:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Direct reproduction} of the prior configuration (eight raw features, RBF kernel,
      a grid over $C$ and $\varepsilon$ only, no scaling), over \RepSeeds{} seeds.
\item \textbf{Equal-budget model comparison}: all \NModels{} models receive identical
      features, identical training rows and an identical tuning budget.
\item \textbf{Corrected statistical inference}: \StatSplits{} identical splits, with the
      Nadeau--Bengio corrected resampled $t$-test as the primary paired test and the
      confidence interval computed correctly.
\item \textbf{Order-independent ablation}: a four-factor full factorial design evaluated in
      all \AblNcells{} cells, with Shapley attribution over all \AblNorders{} orderings.
\item \textbf{Validation under dependence}: spatially blocked cross-validation using
      $k$-means blocks on standardised centroids, plus feature selection repeated inside every
      fold and fully nested estimates.
\item \textbf{External replication}: the workflow applied unchanged to King County house
      sales and Ames Housing.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Key findings}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{A correction to the record.} The value of $R^2 \approx 0.60$ widely associated
      with the prior study's SVR is in fact the score of its linear, ridge and
      polynomial-ridge models. Its SVR-RBF result is $R^2 \approx \PreethiReportedRtwo$. The
      configuration it describes reproduces here at $\RepUnscaledDefault$ with library
      defaults and $\RepUnscaledTuned$ when tuned, bracketing that value; adding a single
      standardisation step to the identical pipeline yields $\RepScaledTuned$.
\item \textbf{Order-independent attribution.} The apparent contribution of scaling varies by
      \RangeScal{} in $R^2$ depending only on where it is placed in the ablation sequence.
      The order-independent Shapley values are $\ShapScal$ (scaling), $\ShapTune$ (tuning),
      $\ShapSelect$ (selection) and $\ShapDeriv$ (derived features). Single-path ablation
      increments are therefore upper bounds, not independent contributions.
\item \textbf{Ranking under equal conditions.} With identical data and an identical tuning
      budget the tuned SVR ranks \SvrRankFull{} of \NModels{}, behind gradient-boosted trees,
      and the paired differences survive the Nadeau--Bengio correction.
\item \textbf{Spatial optimism dominates.} Spatially blocked cross-validation lowers the
      estimate by \SpatialOptimism{} relative to random partitioning --- a larger effect than
      any pipeline component measured in the study.
\item \textbf{Three negative results.} The feature-specific scaling strategy proposed in the
      earlier version of this work does \emph{not} outperform plain standardisation
      ($\CfgManualFull$ against $\CfgUniformFull$ on an identical split); the
      distribution-aware scaler rule introduced to replace it does \emph{not} transfer,
      preserving the component ordering on \NOrderHoldsAuto{} of \NDatasets{} datasets
      against \NOrderHoldsUni{} for plain standardisation and carrying a negative Shapley
      value on Ames ($\DSAmesScal$); and the derived features contribute little once
      selection and tuning are present. All three are reported rather than omitted.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Specific contributions to the AI field}
The contribution is methodological and diagnostic. \emph{No new learning algorithm is
proposed}, and the paper makes no claim to have discovered that kernel methods require
feature scaling --- a result established decades ago and cited as such.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{An order-independent ablation procedure for preprocessing pipelines.}
      Evaluating the full factorial design and attributing components by Shapley value removes
      the dependence of reported increments on the sequence in which components are
      introduced. This is transferable to any staged pipeline and is what revealed that the
      earlier single-path attribution was an artefact.
\item \textbf{A worked correction of a published comparison.} A documented case in which a
      benchmarking result is traced to configuration rather than to algorithm, including the
      correction of a misreading in the earlier version of this work.
\item \textbf{An evaluation protocol for dependent tabular data.} Corrected confidence
      intervals, paired testing on identical splits under the Nadeau--Bengio correction,
      selection inside the resampling loop, and spatially blocked validation --- with the
      magnitude of each source of optimism quantified rather than asserted.
\item \textbf{Fully generated reporting.} Every experiment writes a JSON record, and every
      table, figure and in-text number in the manuscript is generated from those records. No
      value is typed by hand, so text, tables and figures cannot diverge.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Significance and fit for Discover Artificial Intelligence}
The journal's scope covers machine and deep learning, data analytics and AI-enabled
data-driven techniques. Beyond the housing application, the paper addresses a question central
to applied AI: how much of a reported algorithm ranking is a property of the algorithms and
how much is a property of the experimental pipeline around them. The answer given here is
specific and measured --- and, importantly, includes the finding that the validation design
matters more than any preprocessing choice tested.

Conclusions are explicitly restricted to the datasets and configurations examined. The
California Housing dataset ships with scikit-learn, and both additional datasets are on
OpenML, so every result is reproducible by any reader with a Python environment.
